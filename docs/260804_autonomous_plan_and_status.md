## 1. Binding project decisions

### 1.1 Generative-capacity lane

The active theorem programme focuses on whether a guarded act creates new,
reusable public capabilities for forming, transforming, comparing, compiling,
or discharging later obligations.

Work on **semantic novelty** remains on hold. Until an explicit future project
decision resumes it, the project will not:

- extend the semantic-family novelty quotient `Sem`;
- seek positive semantic SR2 provenance or a numerical `nu`;
- mechanize the remaining nonempty semantic demand-orbit or realization cases
  merely to recover a numerical novelty register;
- build new semantic-novelty transcript suites, fixtures, profile gates, or
  experiment readouts;
- use `nu`, semantic SR2, or a semantic-novelty proxy in admission, selection,
  ranking, halting, profile comparison, or weakness adjudication; or
- enlarge the semantic receipt codomain to evade the existing exact negative
  result.

Existing semantic-novelty results remain valid frozen evidence. In particular,
the exact SR2 noninjectivity theorem is retained; it is not weakened,
reinterpreted, or reported as `nu = 0`.

This hold does **not** prohibit semantic typing, normalization, equality,
quotient reconstruction, or subject-path work needed to construct and verify
generative-capacity or task-extension authority. In active work, “semantic” may
describe the meaning of a typed object; “semantic novelty” means specifically
the deferred `Sem`/SR2/`nu` diagnostic.

Terminology for the active lane:

- unqualified **novelty** means generative-capacity gain;
- its carrier is `GCap`;
- its marginal cardinality is `gamma`;
- `nu` appears only when recording the frozen separation theorem or the hold;
  and
- `TaskExt` and `weakness` belong to a separate extension/generalization lane.

### 1.2 Weakest-sufficient law inference

The project adopts the following research-method principle:

> Among candidate law profiles that already satisfy every registered formal and
> empirical constraint, do not prefer a profile that is strictly more specific
> and has a strictly smaller verified extension over the pre-registered space of
> possible parent tasks.

This is an epistemic rule for reverse engineering first principles. It is not
yet an ontological Selective Law. It is inspired by the result that, under a
finite implementable language and a uniform task distribution, maximum
extension (“weakness”) optimizes the probability of generalization. The project
uses the stronger and less prior-dependent extension-inclusion relation as its
primary comparison and treats raw cardinality and weighted priors as secondary
sensitivity analyses.

Weakness is **not**:

```text
minimum kappa
maximum nu
maximum gamma
maximum gamma/kappa
shortest source
fewest axioms by syntax
```

A universal-property formulation may be longer or costlier while remaining
logically weaker because it constrains fewer implementations and applies to
more situations. Conversely, a cheap owner-specific completion may be highly
specific.

### 1.3 Candidate ontological profiles remain separate

The following are different hypotheses and must never be conflated:

- `S0`: total demand-connected discharge;
- `S_W`: extension-maximal discharge;
- `S+`: total discharge plus positive `gamma`;
- `S_up` (written `S↑` in earlier records): total discharge plus strict
  width-two `gamma/kappa` acceleration.

The project has not adopted any one of these as the final Selective Law. Each
must be versioned, reviewed, frozen, tested on non-Genesis cases, and exposed to
the registered prefix only under a common experiment manifest.

## 2. Mission and theorem target

The long-term mission remains to reverse engineer known physics toward the most
likely first principles while preserving the two-law architecture:

1. a constitutive law determines which acts are admissible; and
2. a selective law determines which admissible acts actualize.

The first defensible autonomous target is blind, trace-independent discovery
over a fixed anonymous basis. The engine must:

1. validate the registered Law V2A bootstrap;
2. compile every positive activation from the sealed public grammar;
3. classify present derivability as witnessed derivable, certified
   underivable, or `Unknown`;
4. form live debt only from certified-underivable activations;
5. enumerate the complete finite set of constitutively admissible total
   dischargers in the declared fragment;
6. quotient them by a frozen, target-neutral equivalence;
7. retain every surviving class, preserving branching; and
8. halt only after a complete activation census proves that all activations are
   already derivable, with no unresolved `Unknown` disposition.

Names, expected structures, expected counts, the historical length fifteen,
future-trace viability, target hashes, and presentation-order tie-breakers must
remain outside generation, acceptance, quotienting, selection, and halting.
Recognizing a sealed anonymous output as familiar mathematics or physics is a
held-out decoding step, never an input to the engine.

## 3. Weakest-sufficient hypothesis and extension authority

### 3.1 Why extension belongs in the plan

The project is itself an induction problem. It observes a finite body of
mathematics and physics and seeks principles that will continue to work on
unseen cases. Syntactic economy, low kernel cost, and rapid generative growth
may be useful diagnostics, but none is identical to generalization.

The plan therefore adds a separate extension surface. It asks not merely how
much structure a law or candidate creates, but how broadly the law or candidate
continues to solve target-neutral tasks beyond those used to formulate it.

### 3.2 Validity comes before weakness

Weakness is evaluated only inside a valid-model class. For registered
development evidence `E`, define:

```text
Valid_E(P)
```

to mean that profile `P`:

- satisfies every constitutive and typing requirement;
- reproduces all registered development facts it was required to explain;
- passes the complete generic test and falsifier suite;
- returns `Unknown` rather than inventing answers outside its fragment;
- exposes no oracle labels, expected trace, future survivor, or held-out result;
  and
- has complete theorem authority for every result it claims.

The evidence register `E` is enumerated in the frozen artifact
`docs/260804_validity_fact_register.md`. `E` is identical for all eight
profiles; “required to explain” never varies by profile. Reproduction of the
historical Genesis continuation is explicitly not a member of `E`, and its
absence is never a `Valid_E` failure (§5.1). Each `Valid_E` bullet carries a
decidable check with fail-closed `Unknown` semantics; a `Valid_E` verdict that
cannot be decided is `Unknown`, not exclusion.

A profile that says nothing, accepts everything, or refuses required decisions
is not “weakest sufficient”; it is not sufficient and is excluded before
extension comparison.

### 3.3 Frozen task language

For each extension experiment, freeze a finite target-neutral task language:

```text
TaskManifest =
  (
    vocabulary,
    state/situation grammar,
    decision/realization grammar,
    correctness relation,
    child/parent relation,
    task rank and depth,
    normalization,
    presentation/equivalence quotient,
    resource contract
  )
```

Let `Tasks_V` be the complete finite quotient generated by this manifest. The
vocabulary is part of the hypothesis surface: changing it changes what can be
expressed and therefore changes extension. No task grammar may mention Genesis
stage names, expected mathematical structures, physical labels, expected
candidate counts, or the historical continuation.

The task quotient must be invariant under:

- opaque identifier permutation;
- declaration and enumeration reordering;
- transparent aliases and definitional extensions;
- duplicated syntactic presentations of one task;
- adopted presentation equivalence; and
- transport between independently proven equivalent vocabularies.

### 3.4 Profile extension

For a valid profile `P`, define its verified extension on the frozen task
carrier:

```text
TaskExt_E(P)
  = { tau in Tasks_V |
      P produces a complete correct disposition for tau }
```

Correct disposition means the result required by the task: a verified
construction, a verified negative, or another outcome explicitly declared
correct by the frozen task contract. `Unknown`, resource exhaustion, and
outside-fragment responses do not silently count as successes.

Define extension dominance:

```text
P <=_Ext Q  iff  TaskExt_E(P) subseteq TaskExt_E(Q)
```

Dominance comparison is three-valued and fail-closed. A strict-subset
rejection is licensed only when every task row of both disposition vectors is
decided — verified success or verified non-success under the frozen task
contract. Any `Unknown`, resource-exhausted, or outside-fragment row on either
side makes that pairwise comparison `Unknown`, and no candidate or profile is
ever rejected on an `Unknown` comparison: it survives. Treating an `Unknown`
row as a miss inside a subset comparison would let resource noise act as a
verified negative, violating §5.2.

A valid profile is extension-maximal when no other valid profile has a strict
superset extension. Incomparable profiles remain live hypotheses; no scalar
averaging is permitted to manufacture an ordering.

Under the additional null assumption that tasks are uniformly distributed, the
Bennett-style weakness diagnostic is:

```text
weak_E(P) = |TaskExt_E(P)|
```

Because uniformity is not established for physical problems, raw cardinality is
secondary to exact set inclusion. The programme may also freeze several
weighted priors `pi` and report:

```text
weak_pi,E(P) = sum_{tau in TaskExt_E(P)} pi(tau)
```

A ranking that reverses across reasonable frozen priors is reported as
`WeaknessAdjudicationUnderdetermined`.

### 3.5 Candidate-act extension and `S_W`

At a guarded history `H`, let `D_H` be the complete quotient of
constitutively admissible, demand-connected total dischargers. Freeze a finite
local parent-task carrier `Tasks(H)` from the current public grammar and the
task manifest—not from the actual future trace.

For each `x in D_H`, define:

```text
TaskExt_H(x)
  = { tau in Tasks(H) |
      freely sealing x gives a verified correct realization of tau }
```

The candidate profile `S_W` retains exactly the extension-maximal dischargers:

```text
Accept_SW(H,x)
  iff
    x in D_H
    and not exists y in D_H:
      TaskExt_H(x) proper_subset TaskExt_H(y)
```

`S_W` is a gate over complete dischargers, not a ranking by cardinality. If
several candidates have equal or incomparable maximal extensions, every
inequivalent class survives as a cone.

The derivation function `H -> Tasks(H)` is itself a frozen component of the
shared substrate, and the per-act evaluation of the `|Tasks(H)| x |D_H|`
counterfactual sealings has its own registered resource contract. If that
contract is exhausted, the `S_W` gate returns `Unknown` for the act rather
than a partial rejection, and the three-valued dominance semantics of §3.4
apply to every pairwise comparison.

### 3.6 Separation from `GCap`

`TaskExt` and `GCap` are independent constructions:

- `gamma` counts new irreducible reusable public capabilities created by an act;
- `TaskExt` records which frozen tasks a candidate or profile can solve.

A candidate can enlarge `GCap` without extending the chosen task set, and can
solve additional tasks by recombining old capabilities without creating a new
`GCap` class. No theorem relates `gamma`, weakness, or kernel cost unless one is
proved over the exact carriers.

### 3.7 Held-out physics

Physics-facing evidence enters only after profiles and outputs are sealed.
There are two evaluation layers:

1. target-neutral extension over the frozen internal task carrier; and
2. external held-out mathematical and physical evaluation.

A fact used to formulate or repair a law profile ceases to be held out. Any
successor experiment must reserve new evidence. No post-sealing physical match
may retroactively alter the task language, selector, candidate grammar, or
profile that produced it.

## 4. Formal register separation retained as a lesson

The earlier plan incorrectly treated semantic novelty and generative capacity
as ordered measurements. They are independent finite quotients:

```text
Sem(H,A)  = RawSem(H,A)  / ~sem
GCap(H,A) = RawGCap(H,A) / ~gcap
```

`nu` counts a semantic marginal; `gamma` counts a generative-capability
marginal. No map, inequality, fallback, or substitution between them is
licensed without a future theorem.

The frozen semantic audit found that both registered vectors fail the exact
typed, role-preserving SR2 injection. Therefore positive semantic provenance is
unavailable and `nu` is undefined on those vectors. This is a precise theorem
about `Sem`; it says nothing about `GCap` or `gamma`.

The active quantity is now defined only from the independently constructed
generative lane. If `W_H` is the verified weakening image from the predecessor
quotient, then:

```text
MarginalGCap(H,A) = GCap(H,A) \ W_H
gamma(H,A)        = |MarginalGCap(H,A)|
```

An act may add a reusable integration capability without adding a semantic
distinction. Conversely, a new distinction need not create a reusable public
capability. The project decision is to mechanize and experiment with the first
question.
### 4.1 Three independent quantitative surfaces

The forward programme now distinguishes:

```text
kappa   irreducible public cost
gamma   marginal reusable generative capabilities
weak    extension cardinality under a specified task distribution
```

and the non-scalar extension set:

```text
TaskExt
```

No inequality or monotone map among these surfaces is assumed. In particular,
“weakest” means largest extension among sufficient hypotheses, not lowest
`kappa`; and `gamma/kappa` is a productivity hypothesis, not a theorem about
generalization.

## 5. Non-negotiable boundaries

### 5.1 Oracle and held-out evidence

- The archived Genesis sequence is regression evidence only.
- No production dependency may observe historical names, expected stages,
  expected cardinalities, decoded physical interpretations, or future results.
- Profile hypotheses must be motivated, reviewed, and frozen before live
  Genesis exposure.
- Divergence from the historical sequence is a mathematical result, not an
  automatic software defect.

### 5.2 Authority and evidence

- A digest, Boolean, tag, count, caller assertion, or matching type name is not
  authority.
- Exact bytes must be compared before their digest is recorded.
- Caller-selected roots, capabilities, occurrences, substitutions, receipts,
  or constructor tags may not define a supposedly complete carrier.
- `Unknown` is the required outcome when evidence is incomplete or resources
  fail; it is not permission to guess.
- No incomplete subgate may issue live `gamma`, selection, or bootstrap
  authority.

### 5.3 Finite evidence versus universal law

- Particular checked substitutions do not prove substitution laws.
- A finite occurrence census does not prove generic naturality.
- Universal identity, composition, lifting, preservation, functor, selector,
  and constructor-square laws belong to the generic theorem package.
- Fixed-resource Rust replay proves only successful instances; resource
  exhaustion mints no fact.

### 5.4 History and provenance

- Complete history means complete through an opaque producer-designated
  finalized head, never globally latest or universal EOF.
- Caller events, counts, boundaries, roots, or digests cannot establish history
  completeness.
- Generative provenance uses only the frozen JG1 receipt codomain; semantic SR2
  receipts may not be reused.
- Complete local support is weaker than a globally injective provenance
  assignment; both must be proved separately.

### 5.5 Isolation and versioning

- Evidence-bound root manifests and lockfiles must not be mutated by
  experimental dependencies.
- Generative experiments remain in isolated workspaces.
- Protected wire, outcome, resource, or profile changes require explicit
  versioning, canonical fixtures, layer-specific mutants, and independent
  replay.
- No artifact may be recorded as `Discharged`, `Frozen`, or `Closed` in this
  plan or the architecture ledger until it is committed to version control
  (binding decision 5). Frozen claims are backed by immutable tree hashes,
  and byte-for-byte preservation obligations are checked against the
  committed baseline.
### 5.6 Task-language and extension neutrality

- The task vocabulary, correctness relation, task quotient, priors, and
  extension comparison must be frozen before any profile sees the registered
  prefix.
- A task may not encode the expected next Genesis structure, a later accepted
  branch, or a known physical interpretation.
- Duplicating one task presentation may not enlarge extension.
- A caller-supplied task list or extension count cannot mint completeness.
- Uniform-task weakness and weighted-prior weakness are diagnostics over an
  already verified extension set; neither may replace exact extension
  inclusion.
- `S_W` may not inspect actual future branches. It uses only the frozen local
  parent-task carrier.

### 5.7 Scope and decomposition discipline

- A newly discovered obligation inside an active cut is assigned to the next
  already-named phase boundary unless a short written audit shows it blocks
  the current cut's pre-stated closure (binding decision 6).
- A node that spawns more than two unplanned sub-cuts triggers a mandatory
  scope audit that re-baselines the parent's definition of done before any
  further sub-cut is opened.
- Every remaining QG3/R2d cut has an enumerated closure ledger registered
  before its work starts (§13). A cut is closed exactly when every ledger
  line checks; any residue moves to the next named boundary, never to a new
  sub-alphabet.

### 6.3 Key negative result: full-projection preservation is false

QG3-S1c3 closed the concrete shared typing and denotation of three preservation
subjects. It deliberately did **not** prove them.

For every nonempty action, each normalized primitive contributes a fresh
formal-action occurrence and therefore at least one formal-action key in the
action projection. Those keys cannot occur in the candidate projection. Hence
the current proposition

```text
Full(ProjectSeed) = Full(ReindexedSeed)
```

is uninhabited for every nonempty action. This is a theorem about the proposed
equality's impossibility, not a failure to find its proof.

The exact consequences are:

- candidate and action projections are literally equal only for the empty total
  action;
- normalization remains definitional through the quotient normal-form
  eliminator;
- nontrivial exact-identifier renaming generally requires transport rather
  than literal byte equality (a setwise-projection-stabilizing renaming may
  satisfy the untransported renaming subject; the generic law still requires
  equivariant transport);
- the three stored subjects remain typed proposition syntax storing no
  inhabitant, proof/evidence value, or theorem authority (uninhabitedness is
  proved only for the nonempty full-action equality; the normalization and
  identity-action subjects hold definitionally/literally without any stored
  proof value);
- no compiler, classifier, selector, provenance calculation, `GCap` audit, or
  future proof may assume those equalities merely because the descriptors are
  present; and
- JG2b2b3 universal authority is blocked until a separately versioned theory
  corrigendum is frozen and proved.

This result is methodologically important. The formal-action keys are not
spurious representation noise: they witness genuinely new support introduced
by action. Erasing them to recover equality would destroy the very
action-generated structure that `GCap` is intended to audit.

The corrigendum must cover two distinct laws:

1. **Equivariant renaming:** compare a transported candidate projection with
   the quotient projection reconstructed after renaming; exact identifiers are
   not expected to remain byte-identical.
2. **Action reconstruction with support delta:** compare the independently
   transformed pre-quotient structure with the quotient rebuilt after adding
   the mandatory action-generated raw support. The correct theorem may be a
   transported, restricted, extension, or support-delta law, but it cannot be
   full-projection equality.

The current A4 wire remains valid as a typed subject representation. Its
subjects are historical conjecture syntax until the corrigendum supplies a
different theorem schema or versions their endpoint grammar.

#### 7.2.3 TC-R — equivariant renaming

Literal identifier equality is the wrong theorem under nontrivial renaming.
The theorem target must compare transported structure:

```text
Transport_h(Projection_Q(B))
  = Projection_Q(Rename_Q(B,h))
```

or an explicitly equivalent isomorphism/equivalence formulation.

The transport must cover:

- declarations and global identities;
- binders, referents, owners, and keys;
- normalized contexts, terms, paths, and support;
- classes, coverage, and projection positions;
- lineage and footprints; and
- every retained theorem endpoint.

Required laws:

```text
Transport_id        = identity
Transport_(g o h)   = Transport_g o Transport_h
Transport_h         preserves typing, support, quotient, and coverage
```

Exact byte equality is required only after both endpoints have been rendered in
the same transported naming frame.

#### 7.2.4 TC-A — action reconstruction and support delta

For a nonempty action `q`, full candidate/action projection equality is false.
The replacement theorem should be stated first at the raw reconstructed level:

```text
RawAction(B,q)
  = ActionRebuild(q, RawCandidate(B), DeltaRaw(B,q))
```

where `DeltaRaw(B,q)` is generated independently from the formal action events
and contains every mandatory formal-action occurrence and support edge.
`ActionRebuild` is not assumed to be disjoint union: the frozen corrigendum must
state exactly how inherited material may be transported, merged, removed,
rewritten, or extended.

The quotient endpoint is then reconstructed:

```text
QAction(B,q)
  = Quotient_Q(RawAction(B,q))
```

rather than obtained by filtering or including the old candidate quotient.
This formulation permits the action rebuild to change, merge, remove, and add
quotient classes.

The theorem package must derive, not assume:

- exact raw inherited/action partition or another frozen replacement if the
  partition is not disjoint;
- complete formal-event delta;
- support, lineage, and footprint equations;
- the map from transformed inherited raw occurrences to rebuilt action classes;
- the exact disposition of removed or merged classes;
- current-owner closure behavior;
- identity action:
  `DeltaRaw(B,[]) = empty`, `QAction(B,[]) = QCandidate(B)`, and the
  append-identity `QAction(B, A ++ []) = QAction(B, A)`;
- composition:
  action deltas and rebuilds compose under the frozen action algebra, whose
  carrier, typing, and re-basing rule are themselves TC0 decisions (7.2.2);
- renaming equivariance of both the inherited transform and delta; and
- preservation of typing, normalized endpoints, and resource bounds.

A weaker support-only statement may be adopted if it is the strongest theorem
actually provable, but it must not be mislabeled full preservation.

## 10. Retained lessons that govern future work

1. **`Unknown` is successful safety.** It identifies the missing proof or
   resource without manufacturing authority.
2. **The profile determines the sequence.** A short halt under a narrow profile
   cannot justify retrospective broadening. New constitutive structure needs
   independent motivation and a pre-result freeze.
3. **Semantic novelty and generative capacity are independent.** A negative or
   undefined `nu` has no conclusion about `gamma`.
4. **Support is not injective provenance.** Local support for every member does
   not produce a global receipt injection.
5. **Finite replay is not a universal theorem.** Derivation-local instances and
   generic algebraic laws require different authorities.
6. **Completeness is relative to a verified boundary.** A producer-finalized
   head can delimit a complete finite history; caller EOF/count/digest cannot.
7. **Correspondence requires shared bytes and meaning.** Similar Rust and Agda
   datatypes, tests, or hashes do not prove agreement.
8. **Soundness is not domain completeness.** “Every accepted certificate is
   sound” does not prove that all required subjects were included.
9. **Stability is one-step commutation.** Saturating a finite universe under
   open substitutions is usually the wrong theorem.
10. **Convertible is not exact.** Normal-form equality cannot replace exact
    identity where a protocol binds a declared type, path, field order, or
    digest.
11. **Negative classifications are completeness claims.** Every new input
    family must re-audit the checker’s full failure taxonomy.
12. **Formal executability shapes proof design.** Bounded normalization,
    checked arithmetic, explicit fuel, and fallible allocation are part of the
    theorem boundary.
13. **Each verification layer needs discriminating mutants.** New layers need
    fixtures that earlier layers accept and only the new claim rejects.
14. **Evidence-bound workspaces stay isolated.** Dependency drift must never
    silently alter previously issued evidence.
15. **Typed proposition syntax is not theorem authority.** Formation and
    denotation of an equality subject establish only that a proposition is
    meaningful.
16. **Equivariance is not literal identifier equality.** Renaming the public
    world requires explicit transport of keys, owners, binders, and endpoints.
17. **Nonempty actions are extension-producing.** Formal-action keys are a
    mandatory support delta; a correct action law must reconstruct or relate
    the extended support rather than assert full projection equality.
18. **Weakest is not shortest.** Minimum cost, shortest source, or maximum
    compression does not by itself maximize generalization.
19. **Validity precedes weakness.** A vacuous, noncommittal, or incomplete
    profile is not a sufficient hypothesis and never enters extension
    adjudication.
20. **The vocabulary is part of the experiment.** Task extension is meaningful
    only relative to a frozen implementable language and quotient.
21. **Extension inclusion is primary.** Cardinality and weighted priors are
    diagnostics; incomparable extension sets remain multiple hypotheses.
22. **Generative capacity and task extension are independent.** `gamma` measures
    reusable public capability gain; `TaskExt` measures solved tasks.

## Appendix C. Status glossary

```text
Discharged          the obligation is met and recorded; the record is
                    historical evidence and is not expected to change
Frozen              immutable; any change requires a new version under §5.5
                    and a rerun of every affected closure and mutation suite
Closed (at a stated boundary)
                    complete and immutable at exactly the stated
                    representation or compiler-plan boundary; work beyond
                    that boundary (numerical TypeIds, byte comparison,
                    executable authority) remains open and is assigned to a
                    later named gate
Definition-closed   every definition, encoding, resource bound, and mutation
                    test of the layer is complete; nothing in the layer may
                    change without re-versioning
Draft-not-frozen    formulation-lane text with no authority; may change
                    freely until its registered freeze gate
```

Recording any of the first four statuses in a ledger requires the underlying
artifact to be committed (binding decision 5, §5.5).
