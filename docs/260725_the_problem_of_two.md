# The Problem of Two

**Date:** 2026-07-25.
**Status: SOLVED NEGATIVELY** — see
`docs/260725_the_problem_of_two_negative_resolution.md`. Side A does not imply
Side B from the permitted basis, and the implication stays false even under the
R-α strengthening. A normalized, positive, monotone, permutation-invariant family
computed solely from one binary primitive field has
`I_T(μ_m) = (k!/N^{2m})·{2m brace k}`, nonzero for all `1 ≤ k ≤ 2m`. **The two
sides bound different invariants: Side A bounds primitive-generator arity, Side B
bounds the Boolean polynomial degree of a composite measure, and nonlinear
extraction separates them.** R-α and R-β are dead; R-γ is viable at the cost of a
second root; R-δ needs a faithfulness premise.

**Anticipated in the record, and I missed it.** Campaign amendment 1 item B.5
retired the primeness route partly because "*historical depth-two does not
constrain unary algebraic degree*." That is this result in the one-amplitude
case. §4 below quotes B.5's constraint on the permitted basis while overlooking
that the same item had already declared the target unreachable from it. The
negative resolution generalizes B.5's remark from `|z|^p` to Boolean degree over
`N` alternatives and makes it a theorem.

The text below is retained as the specification the negative resolution was
proved against.

## 1. The problem in one paragraph

The framework's central number appears in two places that the record treats as
independent. On the constitutive side, the coherence-depth theorem bounds the
*arity* of irreducible primitive fields in a public signature at two. On the
operational side, XF-A predicts that every Möbius/Sorkin coefficient of the
public measure vanishes at order three and above. Both say *the public interface
carries no irreducible content above arity two*. The book asserts they have one
root; XF-A registers a derivation obligation to show it. **Prove that the first
implies the second — or show that it cannot.**

---

## 2. The two statements

### 2.1 Side A — constitutive (proved)

In the fixed fully coupled CCHM-style cubical extension calculus, a sealed
structural extension `X` exports an opaque public signature `Σ_X = (K_X, T_X)`.
A trace field is **primitive** when it is not computed by lower-interface data,
**derived** when canonically generated by cubical filler operations from already
exported boundary data. For an obligation `o`, `Supp(o)` is the set of earlier
sealed layers whose exported schemas occur irreducibly in the normalized typing
derivation of `o`, and the **historical arity** is `a(o) = |Supp(o)|`.

> **Theorem (Appendix C).** No irreducible primitive historical field of arity
> `k ≥ 3` survives in a `μ`-minimal public signature. Arity-`k ≥ 3` fields are
> computationally replaceable: Kan operations (`hcomp`, `comp`, `hfill`)
> synthesize their witnesses from lower-arity boundary data, and the replacement
> preserves the canonical normalized signature. Hence `d_μ ≤ 2`, and with the
> recent-history factorization, `d_obl = 2`.

The mechanism: the level-3 horn-extension fibre over a fixed lower boundary is
canonically and computably contractible. Selecting a completion costs zero bits.

### 2.2 Side B — operational (registered, independently derived)

Fix a finite alternative set `A` and one additive event algebra over it, with
public measure `μ : 2^A → ℝ_{≥0}`. Define the Möbius/Sorkin coefficients

```
I_T  =  Σ_{S ⊆ T} (−1)^{|T|−|S|} μ(S) .
```

> **XF-A.** `I_T = 0` for every `|T| ≥ 3`, at every precision, forever.
> Equivalently: the N-path intensity is exactly determined by empty-, one- and
> two-path records.

XF-A **already has a derivation** from conjugate self-pairing alone: if
`μ(S) = |Σ_{i∈S} a_i|²` then each pair contribution carries `(1−1)^{|T|−2} = 0`.
That derivation is not the problem. The problem is the *other* route.

### 2.3 What is to be proved

> **The Problem of Two.** Derive Side B from Side A, using only the permitted
> basis of §4, **without assuming the Born form** `μ(S) = |Σ a_i|²`.

## 3. Why this is one problem and not two

**The book.** Chapter 5 runs the propagation
`two laws ⇒ two obligations ⇒ d_obl = 2 ⇒ two-step memory ⇒ Fibonacci ⇒ φ`, and
says of the later twos that they "are *meant* to be echoes of the same
architectural root." Chapter 37 says the twenty addresses "share a single
source: the two-law architecture and the `d = 2` coherence depth theorem" — but
its Born entry justifies the squared modulus by proof-space cardinality, not by
depth. So the book asserts the common root for the Born case and does not supply
it.

**The campaign.** XF-A is registered with a binding caution that its derivation
obligation may rest *only* on width-two structure and `d_obl = 2`. That
obligation is undischarged.

These are the same task. Closing it would convert the book's most rhetorically
exposed claim into a theorem at precisely the point where a hostile reader
reaches for the word "numerology," and would discharge a registered campaign
obligation. Neither document currently cites the other.

## 4. What may be used, and what may not

The synthesis already fixed the admissible inputs, and the wording is narrow:

> The earlier slogan that Born's exponent, `d²`, `r²`, and Fibonacci are "four
> shadows of ordered self-pairing within the depth-two window" therefore
> **overstates by two**: what survives register-independently is the pair rooted
> directly in width-two structure — the Fibonacci debt recurrence and the depth
> bound `d_obl = 2` — and **any bridge from that depth to the Born exponent must
> be built on those alone.**

**Permitted:** the depth bound `d_obl = 2` in its full content (Level 1
existence, Level 2 structural identity, Level 3 contractible via `isEquiv`
h-propositionality and horn contractibility); the width-two chronological
window; the Fibonacci debt recurrence.

**Barred:** the engine-register `d²` and `r²` counting laws, by name; telescopic

**Fenced with care:** the Fibonacci debt recurrence is on the permitted list,

bridge that consumes the Fibonacci half *and* outputs a physical prediction is
in contact with that fence and must say so.

## 5. Progress: the two statements have the same shape

This is the strongest available reason to think the bridge exists, and it is
better than a coincidence of magnitudes.

**The Sorkin hierarchy is an arity grading.** Möbius inversion gives
`μ(S) = Σ_{T ⊆ S} I_T`, so `I_T` is exactly the *genuinely `|T|`-ary* content of
the measure — the part of `μ` on `T` not already determined by its proper
subsets. Grade-2 says: the measure has no content of arity three or higher.

**Both statements are "the top cell is determined by its boundary."**

| | Side A | Side B |
|---|---|---|
| object | integration fibre over a fixed lower interface boundary | value `μ(T)` given `{μ(S) : S ⊊ T}` |
| claim | fibre is contractible — one filler, zero bits | value is forced by inclusion–exclusion, `I_T = 0` |
| failure mode | genuine primitive data at level 3 | irreducible three-way interference |
| located at | the public signature | the public measure |

Same shape, same failure mode, same interface. This is not two magnitudes that
happen to agree; it is one statement form appearing at one interface in two
registers. That is why the hazard fence does not apply — and why the bridge
ought to exist.

## 6. The attempted derivation, and the counterexample that kills it

### 6.1 The attempt

Read the depth theorem's levels as a statement about arity:

1. **Existence is unary.** A witness is an object, not a number: `ψ : I → A` is
   not a scalar, and `ψψ` does not typecheck. Level 1 cannot produce a public
   quantity on its own.
2. **Identity is binary.** The Level-2 primitive is a comparison of two
   presentations — the book's own gloss, "when two apparently different
   presentations count as the same." Scalar-valued, that is `⟨ψ_i, ψ_j⟩`.
3. **There is no ternary primitive.** Level 3 is contractible.
4. Therefore public scalars are built from binary comparisons, and with coherent
   summation (superposition before extraction, which the synthesis already
   carries) `μ(S) = Σ_{i,j ∈ S} ⟨ψ_i, ψ_j⟩`, whence `I_T = 0` for `|T| ≥ 3` by
   the `(1−1)^{|T|−2}` computation.

### 6.2 Why it fails

**Step 4 does not follow from steps 1–3.** "Built from binary comparisons" does
not entail "additive over the pair decomposition," and only the latter forces
grade-2.

Counterexample, computed: take three alternatives with all pairwise comparisons
`g_ij = 1`.

| measure | form | `I_3` |
|---|---|---|
| additive over pairs | `μ(S) = Σ_{i,j∈S} g_ij` | **0** |
| built from pairs, not additive | `μ(S) = (Σ_{i,j∈S} g_ij)²` | **36** |

The second measure is a function of the binary data *alone* — it uses no
three-way input whatsoever — and it still has irreducible third-order content.
So binarity of the *inputs* is strictly weaker than grade-2 of the *measure*.

The premise the attempt actually needs is additivity of the public weight over
the pair decomposition. **That premise is equivalent to the conclusion.** As it
stands the sketch is circular, and this is the honest verdict on it.

This also corrects §3 of `docs/the_two_2s_arity_bridge_v1.md`, which presented
the sketch without noticing the gap.

## 7. The obstruction, stated exactly

The two arities index different sets.

- **Side A's arity counts earlier sealed layers.** `Supp(o)` is a set of *layers
  of history*. The permitted basis — the width-two chronological window,
  `d_obl = 2` — is indexed over history throughout.
- **Side B's order counts alternatives in one event algebra.** The elements of
  `T` are *simultaneous branches* within a single act, not successive seals.

An alternative is not a layer. Nothing in the record transports an arity bound
over layers to an order bound over alternatives.

Worse, the mechanisms differ in kind. Side A's contractibility is a homotopical
statement about cubical horn-extension fibres in a fixed extension calculus.
Side B's determinacy is a linear-algebraic statement about extending a measure
on a Boolean lattice. Both are "top determined by boundary," but they are
different categories of object, and **no functor between them appears anywhere
in the record.**

That is the problem in one sentence: *the theorem is proved for the wrong index
set, and the two index sets have no established relation.*

## 8. Routes

**R-α — generalize historical arity to interface arity.** Replace "number of
earlier sealed layers" with "support cardinality of an irreducible primitive
interface field over whatever index set," and re-prove computational
replacement for the generalized notion. If the Kan-filler argument uses only the
boundary/top structure and not the *ordering* of layers, the generalization may
be near-routine and the bridge falls out immediately.
*Assessment: most direct, highest payoff. The decisive question is narrow and
answerable by reading Appendix C's proof: does it anywhere use that `Supp(o)` is
a set of temporally ordered layers, or only that it is a finite index set with a
boundary? Answer that first; it costs an afternoon and it gates the route.*

**R-β — realize alternatives as layers.** Exhibit an N-alternative event algebra
as a sealed structure whose alternatives are exported schemas of distinct
layers, so Side A's quantifier reaches them directly.
*Assessment: probably false to the physics — the alternatives of one interference
experiment are not successive seals — but it should be ruled out explicitly
rather than assumed away, because if some restricted class of experiments does
admit such a realization, XF-A could be derived on that class and registered
there.*

**R-γ — split the problem: binarity from Side A, additivity from B.5.** §6's gap
is exactly additivity over the pair decomposition. Campaign amendment 1 item B.5
already registered a route that supplies additivity from elsewhere: Jordan/Hilbert
reconstruction, frame invariance, **orthogonal additivity over public channels**,
continuity, normalization ⇒ Cauchy additivity. If B.5 delivers additivity and
the level structure delivers binarity, grade-2 follows from the two together.
*Assessment: most likely to succeed, and it has the clearest owners. But note the
cost: grade-2 would then rest on B.5 as well as on `d = 2`, so the book's
"single root" claim weakens to "two roots, one of which is `d = 2`." That is a
real and reportable weakening, not a technicality — and it should be decided
in advance whether that outcome counts as success.*

**R-δ — prove the equivalence instead of the implication.** Show that grade-2
implies an arity-2 statement about the public signature, establishing that the
two twos are the same two without deriving either from the other.
*Assessment: weaker, but publishable, and it would license chapter 5's "echo"
language at theorem grade rather than as a hope. Worth doing if R-α and R-γ both
stall.*

## 9. What a solution must satisfy

1. **Verdict-blind.** No measured quantity, and no step motivated by the fact
   that quantum theory satisfies grade-2.
2. **Permitted basis only** (§4). If a solution needs a resource outside it, the
   resource is a new declared premise and must be counted.
3. **Must not assume the Born form.** Grade-2 already follows from it (B.1);
   deriving grade-2 from it is not the problem.
4. **Must survive §6's counterexample** — that is, must explicitly supply
   additivity over the pair decomposition rather than sliding into it from
   binarity. Any proof that does not visibly address this is wrong.
5. **Must state which index set it works over**, and if it generalizes Appendix
   C's arity, must re-prove computational replacement for the generalization
   rather than asserting it transfers.

## 10. What a negative resolution would mean

If the index sets are shown to be genuinely unbridgeable, the outcome is a
result and not a loss:

- **XF-A is unaffected.** It keeps its own derivation from conjugate
  self-pairing and remains registered exactly as it stands.
- **The book narrows honestly.** Chapter 5's "meant to be echoes" becomes the
  *correct final grade* for the Born link rather than a placeholder awaiting
  upgrade, and chapter 37's "single source" needs qualifying for that row.
- **Nothing downstream collapses.** The propagation chain
  `d_obl = 2 ⇒ two-step memory ⇒ Fibonacci` is untouched; only the claim that
  the Born two is the *same* two would fail.

A clean negative is worth publishing, and by the campaign's own discipline it
must be published if found.

---

## 11. Traps

- **Assuming the Born form.** The most likely failure, because the form is so
  familiar that its use goes unnoticed. Any appearance of `|Σ a_i|²` in a step
  is disqualifying.
- **Sliding from binary to additive.** §6 exists to catch this. In the intended
  model the two coincide, which is exactly why the slide is invisible there.
- **Using the engine-register laws.** `d²`, `r²`, telescopic inheritance are
  barred by name, and this is the cluster the "four shadows" slogan was
  retired for.
- **Counting matches.** The case rests on a shared *statement shape at a shared
  interface* (§5), not on two magnitudes both being 2. A solution that argues
  from the numeral has proved nothing.
- **Quietly widening the permitted basis.** §4 is narrow on purpose. Adding
  resources is allowed; adding them silently is not.
- **Treating R-γ's success as full success.** It would split the root in two.
  Decide before starting whether that counts.
