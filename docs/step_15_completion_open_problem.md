## 1. Data that must be defined operationally

Fix a sealed two-step support window `W = (S_(n-1), S_n)` and its typed
signature. The following cannot be prose predicates or caller-provided labels;
each must be an inductive datatype or a decidable kernel judgment with a
replayable derivation.

### 1.1 Typed schema contexts

A context must distinguish at least universe/type parameters, opaque element
parameters, library references, interval variables, and cofibration
assumptions. For dependent cubical schemas, later entries may mention earlier
ones. Context formation, lookup, exchange where legal, weakening, and
substitution must therefore be typed judgments, not raw index rewrites.

### 1.2 Intended depth-two schema grammar

Define a finite grammar `Schema2(W)` whose constructors state exactly which
semantic demands the Two-law program regards as generated by a two-step
window. At minimum the specification must decide, with no retrospective
reference to a desired count:

- ordinary formation, introduction, elimination, and computation families;
- maps and natural transformations;
- adjoint/mate and support-action families;
- coherence/interchange families formed from two composable layers;
- registered HIT/path beta, transport, Kan, face, and overlap families;
- modal and combinatorial-synthesis families already admitted by historical
  extraction;
- whether a construction is a natural family or merely an instance of one;
- when an independently exported demand output creates a distinct orbit.

Every constructor needs formation and typing rules, a depth proof, a support
window, and a semantic interpretation. A raw AST bounded by node depth is not
by itself this grammar.

### 1.3 Typed normalization

For every well-typed schema, define normalization to a stable typed normal
form under the sealed signature. Prove termination, preservation of typing,
and stability under replay. The normal form must record enough provenance to
distinguish a new family from a reference to an existing clause.

### 1.4 Weakening, substitution, and naturality

Define legal typed substitutions between schema contexts, including genuine
typed expression images rather than only sort-identical variable images.
Prove identity, composition, typing preservation, normalization naturality,
and support behavior. State a finite set of naturality generators and prove
that every legal renaming/substitution square in `Schema2(W)` is generated by
them.

The existing Rust audit establishes only the variable-renaming fragment of
this paragraph. The existing Agda mirror likewise does not claim the general
theorem.

### 1.5 Frozen univalent equality

Define the equivalence relation on typed normal forms using exactly the frozen
univalent-equality procedure. Prove it is an equivalence, is a congruence for
the schema constructors, and is respected by weakening, substitution,
normalization, and naturality composition. Equality of display labels or
support-local bucket names is insufficient.

### 1.6 Natural family versus instance

Define a natural family as a typed normal schema together with its parameter
telescope and naturality derivation. Define typed instance membership. Prove
that uniform specialization does not create another family. A specialization
may be separate only when it is exported as a separately identified required
output of a live demand orbit, with that output and its non-reuse recorded.

### 1.7 Marginality and internality

Given the typed predecessor closure, decide whether a natural family is
derivable/internal or marginal. The decision must be invariant under the
frozen equality and under alternative legal presentations of the telescope.
For guarded flows, weakening and erasure need inverse laws:

```text
erase (weaken f) = f
weaken (erase g) = g    up to frozen univalent equality.
```

These laws must carry sufficient provenance to prevent a public token or a
self-imported clause from being silently reclassified as an independent
family.

## 2. The exact classification theorem

Let `Sem2(W)` be well-typed intended schemas modulo typed normalization,
checked weakening/substitution, natural-family instance identification, and
frozen univalent equality. Let `Marg2(W)` be its marginal subquotient after
predecessor internality. Let `SLNF(W)` be the independently defined finite
type of support-local normal forms applicable to `W`.

The desired statement is an explicit equivalence

```text
classify_W : Marg2(W) -> SLNF(W)
realize_W  : SLNF(W) -> Marg2(W)

classify_W (realize_W c) = c
realize_W (classify_W s) = s.
```

The second equation is equality in the typed semantic quotient, not equality
of serialized labels. This theorem decomposes into four obligations:

1. **Coverage.** Every intended, typed, marginal depth-two schema normalizes
   and maps to a support-local form.
2. **Sound realization.** Every admitted support-local form has a typed,
   natural, marginal realization in the same window. If the intended claim is
   only an upper bound, this surjectivity clause should be dropped explicitly
   and the theorem weakened to an injection into `SLNF(W)`.
3. **Faithfulness.** Schemas assigned the same form are equal in the frozen
   semantic quotient; genuinely unequal families are never collapsed because
   they share a coarse syntactic shape.
4. **Naturality completeness.** The finite generator audit covers every legal
   typed context morphism relevant to the quotient, not only permutations of
   existing raw parameters.

The distinction in item 2 is important. The current semantic normalization
program asks only for a one-way, proof-carrying upper-bound classifier and
explicitly does not assume saturation of the old support-local codes. An
*exact* classification is stronger. It should be adopted only if realization
and both inverse laws are actually proved. A failed realization is evidence
against exactness, not permission to remove the code from `SLNF(W)` after the
fact.

## 3. Required class-by-class proof

Schema 3 contains three finite strata but its rows are projections of
candidates, not proofs that the semantic domain is exhausted. For every class
that occurs in those rows, the bridge must provide:

- the relevant `Schema2` constructors;
- a total typed extractor from candidate derivations;
- normalization and equality witnesses;
- the complete generator list and an induction showing exhaustiveness;
- support-local classification and faithfulness;
- predecessor marginality/internality;
- a proof that every schema-3 row in the class is joined to the resulting
  theorem without importing forbidden fields.

This includes Foundation, Former, Map, Axiomatic, Modal, HIT/V2, Synthesis,
and any currently Unknown row. “Unknown” may end only by a typed
classification or a named obstruction; it cannot be dropped or averaged.

For HIT/V2/V3 rows, the induction additionally requires typed interval and
cofibration contexts, typed `coe`/`hcom`, a motive-typed `PathCon` eliminator,
and face/overlap substitution laws. All four registered diagrams are now
term-typed under v3. S1, S2, and S3 have exact-prefix constant-boundary
realizer bundles, but no independence or exhaustiveness theorem. Trunc is the
separating case: its typed `x/y` boundary has no endpoint-sensitive
`PathCon` beta/Kan realizer. A complete proof must solve that term layer and
then prove the classifier; neither registered typing nor finite cardinality
may stand in for semantic exhaustiveness.

## 5. Suggested proof strategy

1. Start with an intrinsically typed Agda definition of contexts, substitutions,
   and `Schema2`, so ill-scoped or ill-sorted schemas are unrepresentable.
2. Give a structurally recursive normalizer with a decreasing depth/fuel
   measure. Export only replayable finite certificates to Rust.
3. Prove substitution and normalization lemmas by induction on typing
   derivations, extending the restricted `KernelBridge.agda` results.
4. Present context morphisms by elementary generators—typed adjacent
   exchanges, weakening, typed instance substitution, and the cubical face
   maps—and prove a decomposition theorem.
5. Define support-local forms independently of the historical answer and
   implement `classify`. Search for counterexamples to faithfulness before
   attempting `realize`.
6. Prove the inverse laws class by class. Preserve any failed class as a named
   obstruction in schema 4.
7. Export compact theorem tokens whose replay recomputes typing,
   normalization, generator decomposition, equality, and provenance.
8. Only then regenerate the candidate-level artifact and run the full
   mutation battery.

This order permits a useful one-way theorem even if exact classification is
false: a proved injection `Marg2(W) -> SLNF(W)` can support an upper bound
without pretending every finite code is realized.

## 6. Machine acceptance criteria

The obstruction may be cleared only when all of the following pass:

- Agda checks with `--safe` and without postulates, rewrite axioms, or K;
- every grammar constructor has a typed realization and normalization rule;
- exhaustive finite tests cover all schema-3 rows in all three strata;
- property tests cover identity, composition, weakening, typed instance
  substitution, and normalization naturality;
- generator decomposition replay succeeds for every emitted naturality
  witness;
- presentation changes and frozen univalent equality preserve family IDs;
- altered types, motives, substitutions, equalities, supports, demand-orbit
  outputs, and provenance hashes fail replay;
- a semantic family not generated by the claimed basis is either impossible
  by the induction or becomes a concrete counterexample—never an ignored row;
- the create-new successor replay changes the C-2 status only from the new
  theorem token, with all other open obligations remaining independently
  fail-closed and every archival schema remaining exact.

## 7. Falsifiers and honest outcomes

Any of the following refutes the exact theorem as stated:

- two inequivalent marginal semantic families map to one support-local form;
- an intended typed schema has no support-local normal form;
- a support-local code has no typed marginal realization when surjectivity is
  claimed;
- a legal typed substitution is not generated by the alleged finite basis;
- classification changes under legal weakening, telescope presentation, or
  frozen univalent equality;
- resolving a boundary requires an unbound term or an uncharged new clause.

On such a counterexample, retain the witness and revise the theorem. The most
plausible conservative successor is the one-way provenance classifier already
described in `SEMANTIC_NORMALIZATION_PROGRAM.md`, rather than an exact
support-local isomorphism.
