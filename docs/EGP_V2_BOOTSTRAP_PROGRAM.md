# EGP-v2 bootstrap preregistration

**Registered:** 2026-07-18.

**Status:** specification frozen before the EGP-v2 Stage 1--4 run; no EGP-v2
outcome is recorded in this document.

This is a new Selective-Law experiment prompted by the certified failure of
EGP-v1. It does not amend, replay, or reinterpret that result. The immutable
EGP-v1 burned artifact is `docs/semantic_reselection.json`:

```text
bytes  = 4107
sha256 = 4F6669F8A11554E8A1B7982CDCFF64C82B280EA5A60B348A4736407D1F60AFC4
```

EGP-v1 re-enacted the legacy Universe candidate at Stage 1 but scored it `2`
instead of `1`; its Stage-2 cone then contained only the old universe
application presentation of Unit, which was internal and scored `0`. Those
facts remain evidence against EGP-v1. An EGP-v2 artifact must use a new path,
schema version, law identifier, and digest. It must never overwrite the v1
artifact.

## 1. Frozen semantic change

EGP-v2 counts a canonical **independent generative basis**, not every marginal
normalized family. For history `H` and candidate `x`, let `Sch2(H)` be the
validated depth-two natural families and let `FreeCl_H` be the proof-carrying
closure generated by the rules frozen below. The extractor must produce a
finite set `G_H(x)` such that

```text
Sch2(I(H,x)) = FreeCl_H(wk Sch2(H) ∪ G_H(x)).
```

Every family outside `G_H(x)` must carry a replayable derivation from the
right-hand side. Every generator must be indispensable:

```text
g ∉ FreeCl_H(wk Sch2(H) ∪ (G_H(x) \ {g}))    for every g ∈ G_H(x).
```

To prevent a convenient but arbitrary basis choice, `G_H(x)` is the
least-cardinality covering subset of the finite canonical family inventory;
ties are resolved by the frozen ordering of semantic family IDs. Coverage,
minimum cardinality, the tie-break, and indispensability are certificate
fields, not assertions supplied by a caller.

`FreeCl_H` contains only registered, typed rules:

- checked normalization and computation;
- checked weakening and renaming;
- the frozen univalent-equality procedure;
- substitution and natural-family instantiation;
- completion rules attached to a typed declaration kind, including the
  universe-package and sealed one-constructor-inductive completions below.
  Universe completion is scoped to the *same checked universe declaration and
  package certificate*; there is no generic rule deriving `App(Univ,_)` from
  any family containing `Univ`.

Each closure edge records its rule and premises. Neither a stage number,
historical label, old score, current bar, nor desired winner is available to
the closure or basis algorithm.

## 2. Fresh typed declarations

The candidate language must distinguish a fresh exported declaration from the
expression that classifies it. A declaration has at least:

```text
FreshDecl {
  head_id,          // generative binder identity, alpha-renaming invariant
  classifier,       // a typed expression in the predecessor context
  declaration_kind,
  export_state,
  signature_data
}
```

Normalization may rename a head but may not replace it with its classifier.
Thus a fresh `Unit : U0` is not the expression `App(Univ,m)`. Conversely,
freshness is not sufficient for credit: marginality and basis extraction still
test the declaration's semantic content against predecessor closure and
univalent equality. Re-exporting an already derivable or equivalent structure
therefore receives no new basis atom.

The declaration kind, classifier, signature, and universal-property data are
typed inputs. Display names such as `Universe`, `Unit`, and `Witness` have no
semantic or scoring authority.

## 3. Registered bootstrap signatures

### Stage 1: universe package

The universe package has one independently generated kernel atom. Its decoding
or application family is covered by the registered universe-package completion
and earns no second point unless that coverage derivation fails. The Stage-1
acceptance test is therefore not the numeral `1` as an exception: it is the
machine obligation that a one-atom basis covers both typed operational
families, with the second family derived by the generic completion rule.

### Stage 2: Unit formation

The intended candidate introduces an open one-constructor inductive signature:

```text
fresh inductive-head Unit : U0
constructor-slots         = Fin 1
signature-state           = open
```

Acceptance exports exactly one live semantic demand orbit,
`Constructor(Unit,0)`. The orbit is tied to `Unit`'s fresh head and cannot be
answered by a generic point of another type. At this stage “Unit” means
**Unit formation** or **proto-Unit**; terminality has not yet been claimed.

The required Stage-2 checks are:

1. `Unit` is a typed fresh export, not a normalized spelling of
   `App(Univ,m)`;
2. its semantic content is not in the Stage-1 free closure;
3. its canonical basis has one fresh atom;
4. exactly one constructor-output orbit is live after acceptance.

### Stage 3: constructor discharge and sealing

The intended candidate exports

```text
fresh constructor star : El(Unit)
answers                 = Constructor(Unit,0)
```

It must answer that exact live orbit once. Once the sole slot is filled, the
signature seals and the registered free completion generates the Unit
eliminator and beta law:

```text
unit-ind  : C(star) -> Π (x : El(Unit)), C(x)
unit-beta : unit-ind(c, star) = c.
```

The generated eliminator and beta family receive zero additional credit when
their completion derivations replay. If an implementation instead finds an
independently exported, non-derivable family, it may count only as a distinct
basis atom with its own valid provenance anchor. The historical Stage-3 score
is not assumed. No second constructor may be added after sealing.

After sealing, induction and beta distinguish Unit from an arbitrary pointed
type. Contractibility or terminality is a derived theorem only when the
ambient identity, function, and extensionality fragment is sufficient to
derive it; a fresh type plus `star` alone must not be labelled terminal.

### Stage 4: uniform application of the same law

The complete Stage-4 cone is reselected using exactly the same declaration,
closure, basis, demand, and provenance rules. Pi/Sigma labels do not activate a
special rule and the legacy score is not a target. Any generated projections,
applications, or beta families require explicit free-completion derivations;
any remaining independent exports require separate basis atoms and anchors.

At every stage, semantic variants are expanded over the complete enumerated
and admitted cone before scoring: a plain variant; a one-nullary formation
variant for every structurally eligible one-clause candidate when no
declaration is live; and the exact constructor-completion variant when one
slot is live. Variants are never attached after enumeration to a legacy
reference candidate or selected by a reference hash, stage label, or desired
winner.

## 4. EGP over credited atoms

Only atoms in `G_H(x)` are credited. Each atom must inject, with dependent
anchor evidence, into

```text
ProvTag = (KernelClause × LocalRole)
        ⊎ Σ (o : LiveDemandOrbit), RequiredOutput(o),

LocalRole = KernelHead | AdjointMate | SupportAction | Coherence.
```

A demand answer counts only when it is itself an independent basis atom and is
exported at the named required position of a pre-existing live orbit. Uniform
specializations are not multiplied. Generated completion families are not
credited. Independent exports must be separately charged or answer distinct
live outputs. Injectivity, exact anchor validity, demand liveness, window
locality, and non-reuse all fail closed.

Fresh-declaration evidence is stateless: it proves the local formation or
completion judgment but does not prove that a demand remains live. The
sequential semantic ledger must independently certify that the constructor
slot is live before completion, consume that exact slot once, record the
signature sealed afterward, and reject every attempted second fill. Each stage
chains the preceding ledger/certificate digest. A completing constructor is
one basis atom with one provenance tag (a demand anchor or a local-role anchor),
never both and never double-counted merely because it discharges an orbit.

Consequently the revised score is

```text
nu_v2(H,x) = |G_H(x)|,
```

after all basis atoms have valid EGP anchors. A candidate with an uncovered
family, invalid basis proof, or unanchored atom is semantically invalid rather
than assigned a convenient partial score.

## 5. Forbidden repairs

The following invalidate the EGP-v2 run:

- stage-specific score constants, bonuses, penalties, or equality rules;
- consulting the legacy winner, `nu`, bar, or desired Stage-15 history while
  extracting families, selecting a basis, or assigning provenance;
- treating display labels as declaration kinds or proof evidence;
- weakening semantic equality merely to separate Stage 2 from Stage 1;
- counting a free specialization, generated eliminator, beta rule, or
  coherence family without an independent basis and anchor proof;
- silently changing a closure, declaration, demand, or tie-break rule after
  any Stage 1--4 result has been observed.

Any such change creates EGP-v3 and requires another preregistration and a new
artifact; it cannot repair the EGP-v2 burned result.

## 6. Burned Stage 1--4 decision gate

Before execution, freeze and record at least the source revision and hashes of
the AST schema, declaration rules, type rules, normalization/equality kernel,
free-completion rules, family extractor, basis algorithm and ordering, demand
rules, EGP anchors, candidate enumerator, initial state, and sealed legacy
comparison trace. The semantic run must not read the legacy comparison fields.

Starting from the empty semantic history, enumerate and score the complete
admissible cone sequentially. Commit each stage's raw result and updated demand
ledger before deriving the next bar. Stop and publish the complete prefix if a
stage has no clearing candidate. Otherwise stop after Stage 4. Only then run a
separate comparison pass against the sealed legacy trace.

The first complete execution is burned regardless of outcome. Its artifact
must contain these result fields; this preregistration assigns none of their
values:

```text
law_id, schema_version, frozen_hashes, burned_run, run_digest
outcome.kind, outcome.stage, first_divergence

stages[]:
  stage, bar_before
  cone_enumerated, cone_admitted, cone_deduped, cone_digest
  candidates[]:
    candidate_id, typed_declarations, natural_families
    predecessor_closure, basis_atoms
    coverage_derivations, minimum_basis_certificate
    indispensability_derivations, tie_break_certificate
    live_orbits_before, orbit_answers, live_orbits_after
    provenance_tags, anchor_derivations
    kappa, revised_nu, rho, clears_bar, rejection
  order, winner, ledger_transition, bar_after

posthoc_comparison[]:
  legacy_winner_id, winner_semantically_equivalent
  legacy_nu, score_matches_legacy
  legacy_bar_before, bar_before_matches_legacy
  legacy_bar_after, bar_after_matches_legacy

bootstrap_invariants:
  stage1_one_atom_covers_universe_package
  stage2_fresh_unit_atom
  stage2_one_live_constructor_orbit
  stage3_star_answers_that_orbit
  stage3_signature_sealed
  stage3_generated_unit_ind_and_beta_covered
  alpha_renaming_invariant
  univalent_representations_do_not_mint_atoms
```

In the preregistered-but-not-run state, outcome, winner, score, comparison, and
invariant-result values are absent or `null`. The gate passes only if every
certificate replays and all bootstrap invariants are true. Re-enactment of the
legacy Stages 1--4 is reported only if the post-hoc winner, score, and bar
comparisons all succeed. A divergence is a result, not permission to retune.

Passing this bootstrap gate authorizes a separately preregistered Stage 5--15
semantic reselection. It does not by itself prove the Genesis history or the
global halt at fifteen.
