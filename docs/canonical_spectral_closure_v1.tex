\section{Canonical Spectral Closure and Dynamical Self-Accounting}
\label{sec:canonical-spectral-closure}

Primitive-Parameter Provenance excludes unexplained continuous coefficients, but it does
not say which endogenous certificate should relate the gauge, Yukawa, scalar, and
gravitational parts of the action.  The preceding reconstruction has already produced a
positive state--effect metric, complex finite quantum kinematics, a dagger process theory,
a metric/connection/curvature shell, and a carrier-bearing Standard Model alphabet.  A
physical realization can nevertheless use one structure to define states and geometry and
then attach an unrelated list of numerical weights to its dynamics.  That would leave the
main constants problem untouched.

The next physical closure principle is the demand that the geometry account for its own
dynamics:
\begin{center}
\emph{The same operator that determines what can propagate, how it is measured, and which
internal distinctions exist must generate the relative weights by which those distinctions
act.  A second independent coefficient ledger is not a completed explanation.}
\end{center}

This is the numerical analogue of free sealing and ledger invariance.  Free sealing forbids
unforced generators and equations; Primitive Public Irredundancy forbids idle distinctions;
Reversible Determination supplies a canonical positive trace metric; Isometric Ledger
Invariance forbids a lossless recoding from changing the account.  Canonical Spectral
Closure applies the same discipline to the primitive action.

\begin{definition}[Real even spectral realization]
A real even spectral realization of the completed finite physical API is a tuple
\[
 (\mathcal A,\mathcal H,D,J,\gamma)
\]
in which $\mathcal A$ acts faithfully on the complex Hilbert carrier $\mathcal H$,
$D=D^\dagger$ has the selected spacetime and finite internal parts, $J$ is the real
structure, and $\gamma$ is the grading.  Inner fluctuations have the form
\[
 D_A=D+A+JAJ^{-1},
 \qquad
 A=\sum_k a_k[D,b_k].
\]
The finite part of $D$ carries the Yukawa and mass operators; its commutators and inner
fluctuations carry the gauge and scalar geometry.
\end{definition}

\begin{axiom}[Canonical Spectral Closure]
\label{ax:canonical-spectral-closure}
Whenever the completed ordinary matter--geometry API has a real even spectral realization,
its primitive Euclidean action is the canonical functional of that same fluctuated operator,
\[
 \boxed{
 S_{\rm phys}[\Psi,D_A]
 =\frac12\langle J\Psi,D_A\Psi\rangle
  +\operatorname{Tr} f(D_A/\Lambda),
 }
\]
where $f$ is an even positive spectral profile and $\Lambda$ is a matching scale.  No
independent factor-specific gauge weights, overall Yukawa normalization, scalar quartic,
nonminimal scalar-curvature coefficient, or curvature-squared coefficient may be inserted
in addition to the coefficients generated by this functional.  Departures at lower scales
must arise from renormalization-group evolution, and departures at the matching interface
must be carried by an explicit threshold record or by an independently demanded additional
carrier.

The profile $f$, its moments, and the placement of $\Lambda$ are themselves subject to
Primitive-Parameter Provenance.  The axiom therefore fixes relative coefficients and
boundary relations; it does not license an arbitrary cutoff profile or matching scale as a
self-certifying input.
\end{axiom}

\begin{remark}[Placement in the book architecture]
Canonical Spectral Closure is a physical completion axiom in the \emph{numerical
determinacy} package.  Its natural order is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \text{Primitive-Parameter Provenance}
 &\Longrightarrow \text{Canonical Spectral Closure}\\

\end{aligned}

The first principle says that numbers need certificates.  The present principle supplies one

\end{remark}

\subsection{Heat-kernel form and the remaining spectral moments}

For a four-dimensional almost-commutative realization, the bosonic action has the asymptotic
form
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}f(D_A/\Lambda)
 \sim
 f_4\Lambda^4 a_0(D_A^2)
 +f_2\Lambda^2 a_2(D_A^2)
 +f_0 a_4(D_A^2)+\cdots,
\]
where $f_4,f_2,f_0$ are moments of the spectral profile.  The dimension-four coefficient
$f_0$ controls the relative normalization of gauge, scalar, and curvature-squared terms;
$f_2\Lambda^2$ and $f_4\Lambda^4$ enter the Newton and cosmological terms.  Canonical
Spectral Closure therefore makes the dimensionless sector highly predictive while leaving
an honest residual problem: the spectral profile and the scale moments need their own
provenance certificate.

\subsection{Gauge normalization and the triple-boundary test}

\begin{theorem}[Spectral gauge relation]
\label{thm:csc-gauge-relation}
For the already selected one-generation Standard Model representation, repeated over the
sealed generations, canonical normalization of the gauge kinetic terms gives
\[
 g_3^2(\Lambda)=g_2^2(\Lambda)=\frac53 g_Y^2(\Lambda).
\]
With $g_1=\sqrt{5/3}\,g_Y$, this is
\[
 \boxed{g_1(\Lambda)=g_2(\Lambda)=g_3(\Lambda)=:g_*}.
\]
The common spectral moment is
\[
 \boxed{f_0=\frac{\pi^2}{2g_*^2}},
\]
and the weak mixing angle at the spectral boundary is
\[
 \boxed{\sin^2\theta_W(\Lambda)=\frac38}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proofsketch}
The coefficient of each gauge kinetic term is the canonical trace of the squared generator
over the complete fermionic carrier.  The representation indices give the usual
$5/3$ hypercharge normalization, while one common $f_0$ multiplies all three terms.  Setting
each kinetic coefficient to $1/4$ yields
$g_3^2f_0/(2\pi^2)=1/4$ and the displayed relations
\cite{chamseddine-connes-marcolli}.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal-SM triple-boundary failure]
\label{prop:csc-gauge-threshold}
At the manuscript's independently recomputed two-loop electroweak equal-trace crossing,
\[
 \mu_X=1.08939\times10^{13}\ {\rm GeV},
 \qquad
 g_1(\mu_X)=g_2(\mu_X)=0.545701,
 \qquad
 g_3(\mu_X)=0.580413.
\]
Canonical Spectral Closure would require a strong-sector matching correction
\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta\!\left(\frac1{g_3^2}\right)_*
 =\frac1{0.545701^2}-\frac1{0.580413^2}
 =0.389653.
 }
\]
Equivalently, the uncorrected strong coupling is $6.361\%$ above the common value and must
be reduced by $5.981\%$.  The two inferred spectral moments are
\[
 f_0^{(12)}=16.57144,
 \qquad
 f_0^{(3)}=14.64858,
\]
a relative mismatch of $-11.603\%$.  Hence the minimal Standard Model has no exact
spectral gauge boundary at this crossing.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proofsketch}
Substitute the audited two-loop couplings in the preceding theorem.  The result is a matching
obligation, not evidence for choosing the crossing after seeing the discrepancy.  A lawful
completion must derive the correction from an independently selected threshold sector or
replace the asymptotic cutoff realization by a spectrally justified alternative.
\end{proofsketch}

\subsection{Yukawa norm and fermion--boson mass relation}

Define the positive trace invariants
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A_Y={}&\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
 Y_\nu^\dagger Y_\nu+Y_e^\dagger Y_e
 +3Y_u^\dagger Y_u+3Y_d^\dagger Y_d
 \right),\\
 B_Y={}&\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
 (Y_\nu^\dagger Y_\nu)^2+(Y_e^\dagger Y_e)^2
 +3(Y_u^\dagger Y_u)^2+3(Y_d^\dagger Y_d)^2
 \right).
 \end{aligned}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Spectral Yukawa sum rule]
\label{thm:csc-yukawa-sum}
Canonical normalization of the Higgs kinetic term gives
\[
 \boxed{A_Y(\Lambda)=4g_*^2.}
\]
Equivalently, for running masses defined in one common scheme at the same scale,
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\rm gen}
 \left(m_\nu^2+m_e^2+3m_u^2+3m_d^2\right)
 =8M_W^2.
 }
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proofsketch}
The finite Dirac operator enters both the fermionic Yukawa term and the bosonic Higgs kinetic
term.  Rescaling the Higgs to canonical normalization fixes the total squared Yukawa norm,
rather than allowing an independent overall Yukawa coefficient
\cite{chamseddine-connes-marcolli}.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{corollary}[Top-dominance and equal-neutrino limits]
If the top Yukawa dominates the trace, then
\[
 3y_t^2=4g_*^2,
 \qquad
 \boxed{\frac{y_t}{g_*}=\frac2{\sqrt3}=1.154700538\ldots},
\]
and therefore
\[
 \boxed{\frac{m_t}{M_W}=\sqrt{\frac83}=1.632993162\ldots}
\]
at the same running scale.  If instead one Dirac-neutrino Yukawa equals the top Yukawa and
both dominate, then
\[
 y_t=y_\nu=g_*,
 \qquad
 \boxed{\frac{m_t}{M_W}=\sqrt2=1.414213562\ldots}.
\]
The second limit is included as a structural comparison.  It is not compatible with a
sub-eV neutrino mass under the current one-Higgs Dirac realization without an additional
mass-suppression mechanism.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The Higgs quartic and a no-intersection theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Spectral quartic relation]
\label{thm:csc-quartic}
In the Standard Model convention
\[
 V(H)=-m^2H^\dagger H+\lambda(H^\dagger H)^2,
\]
Canonical Spectral Closure gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \lambda(\Lambda)=4g_*^2\frac{B_Y}{A_Y^2}.
 }
\]
For three generations, regarding the color multiplicities as repeated positive entries,
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \frac1{24}\le\frac{B_Y}{A_Y^2}\le1,
\]
and hence
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{g_*^2}{6}\le\lambda(\Lambda)\le4g_*^2.
 }
\]
In the top-dominance limit,
\[
 \boxed{\lambda(\Lambda)=\frac43g_*^2};
\]
with one equal top and neutrino Yukawa,
\[
 \boxed{\lambda(\Lambda)=g_*^2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proofsketch}
The $a_4$ heat-kernel coefficient contains the fourth Yukawa moment $B_Y$, while canonical
normalization of the Higgs kinetic term contributes two powers of $A_Y^{-1}$.  The stated
normalization is the one in which the observed Standard Model quartic is approximately
$0.13$ at the electroweak scale.  The lower bound follows from
$(\sum_{i=1}^{24}x_i)^2\le24\sum_i x_i^2$ for the nonnegative squared Yukawa entries
\cite{chamseddine-connes-marcolli}.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{theorem}[Minimal one-Higgs Dirac spectral no-intersection, one-loop diagnostic]
\label{thm:csc-minimal-no-intersection}
Use the measured one-Higgs Standard Model boundary data near the top mass and evolve the
one-loop equations with the top Yukawa retained.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em]
\item the weakest possible three-generation spectral quartic bound,
      $\lambda=g_2^2/6$, is crossed at
      \[
       \mu_{\rm min}=1.10893\times10^4\ {\rm GeV};
      \]
      above that scale the running Standard Model quartic lies below every admissible
      one-Higgs spectral value;
\item the top-dominance Yukawa sum rule $3y_t^2=4g_2^2$ is reached only at
      \[
       \mu_Y=2.97589\times10^5\ {\rm GeV},
      \]
      where
      \[
       g_2=0.610602,
       \quad y_t=0.705063,
       \quad \lambda_{\rm SM}=0.0358719,
      \]
      while the spectral quartic is
      \[
       \lambda_{\rm spec}=\frac43g_2^2=0.497114,
      \]
      a factor $13.858$ larger;
\item in the illustrative equal-$y_t$--$y_\nu$ limit, $y_t=g_2$ occurs at
      \[
       \mu_{Y\nu}=1.56648\times10^9\ {\rm GeV},
      \]
      where $\lambda_{\rm SM}=-0.0062746$ while
      $\lambda_{\rm spec}=g_2^2=0.330490$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the current minimal one-Higgs Dirac Standard Model has no common one-loop
scale satisfying both the spectral Yukawa normalization and the spectral Higgs quartic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proofsketch}
The numerical integration uses $g_1=\sqrt{5/3}g_Y$ and the one-loop Standard Model beta
functions, initialized with the high-order weak-scale matching formulas of
\cite{buttazzo-criticality}.  Item (i) is stronger than the top-only comparison: once
$\lambda<g_2^2/6$, no distribution of the 24 nonnegative Yukawa entries can satisfy the
spectral quartic relation.  In the current Dirac-neutrino realization the observed light
fermion Yukawas are negligible compared with $y_t$, so the top-dominance crossing is the
relevant minimal test.  The calculation is a reproducible diagnostic, not a replacement for
a full multi-loop threshold analysis.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{corollary}[Canonical spectral and critical boundaries cannot coincide]
\label{cor:csc-cba-separation}
For a nontrivial Yukawa sector, Canonical Spectral Closure gives
\[
 \lambda_{\rm spec}=4g_*^2\frac{B_Y}{A_Y^2}>0,
\]
whereas Critical Boundary Actualization gives
\[
 \lambda_{\rm crit}=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\mu_{\rm spec}\ne\mu_{\rm crit}}
\]
in a nontrivial threshold-free realization.  If the two descriptions refer to one physical
interface, an explicit threshold correction must interpolate between them.
\end{corollary}

minimal intersection failure while making new numerical predictions.  It is not currently

violate verdict-blindness.  The singlet becomes lawful only if an independently stated
