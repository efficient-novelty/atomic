presentations: two application orders for each of the two former

## Finite completeness boundary

`GF2` means **Genesis Fragment, depth two**; it is not finite-field notation.
The first executable completeness target is a versioned finite GF2
artifact whose admitted language contains exactly:

- finite dependent contexts and normalized telescopes;
- the trace's finite universe levels;
- `Pi`, `Sigma`, and finite sums with their declared computation rules;
- the finite path/cubical operations required by the trace;
- sealed opaque constants referenced by digest;
- finite candidate-API equation sets;
- registered depth-two demand schemes; and
- a finite grammar of equivalence witnesses used by the quotient checker.

GF2 excludes unrestricted recursion, higher-order unification, and arbitrary
equivalence search. The required closure theorem must prove that every
positive-cost, demand-connected, constitutively admissible total discharger
in scope has a representative generated inside this finite fragment. That
closure theorem is a target, not an implemented result.

The artifact must enumerate the complete carrier, closure rules, equivalence
witness grammar, canonical order, and digest. Completeness claims are relative
to that artifact:

- an admitted input may receive a positive or negative certified decision;
- an input outside the carrier returns `Unknown(OutsideFragment)`;
- an exhausted budget returns `Unknown(ResourceExhausted)`; and
- neither `Unknown` result may become `Blocked`, uniqueness, inequivalence, or
  halt.

The repository now implements the versioned GF2 feature/limit manifest, its
canonical digest, four-way decision boundary, and a native adapter for the
existing dependent-core subset. Manifest validation proves ordering, bounds,
inventory identity, and backend binding only. It does not certify the listed
inventories as sound or complete, and the finite carrier, cubical semantics,
equivalence grammar, and closure/completeness theorem remain unimplemented.

## Bootstrap and debt-free behavior

Law V2A starts from a disclosed, registered three-act bootstrap. It does not
claim that those acts are derived or unique.

Law V2B is the later least-arena theorem: derive the founding prefix from the
empty public context and prove uniqueness up to the adopted equivalence. That
theorem is open.

For post-bootstrap history, positive-cost Constitutive admissibility includes
demand-connectedness. Once a complete census proves the live obligation set
empty, no positive-cost continuation is admissible. Zero-cost theorem
readouts do not append history. `rho <= 4` remains a diagnostic inequality and
has no selection or halt authority.
