Needs to be added to the First Law: *Contectual internalization* 
The previous version of the Two laws resulted in a strict replay in creating the Direct Elimator as step 4, instead of the anticipated Pi/Sigma structure.
The direct-eliminator result may be telling us that the H3 profile operated at the wrong level of abstraction.

It treated the unit type as an ordinary finite inductive datatype. Given that interpretation, the direct eliminator is exactly the correct next structure. The current Constitutive Law chapter practically predicts it:

Introduce a kind of thing, and the world owes the means of using or eliminating instances of that kind.

That is precisely the debt the H3 profile compiled.

But the same book says that the first three acts are not ordinary candidate structures. They are arena-founding acts: they establish the field in which later selection becomes possible. If the universe, unit type, and witness are the founding of a logical arena rather than merely the first three data declarations in an already complete metalanguage, then their principal debt may be larger than owner-specific unit elimination.

That opens a principled route to contextual internalization.

The key philosophical principle

I would formulate it as:

Constitutive Self-Containment: No operation that is essential to the formation, substitution, or comparison of public possibilities may remain permanently external to the world once it acts nontrivially on sealed public structure.

Or more memorably:

The grammar must eventually become part of the world it grammars.

This follows naturally if “logic is fundamental” means explanatory closure rather than merely logical priority:

The Constitutive Law uses contexts, context extension, substitution, and reindexing to state what is well formed and what follows from what.
If those operations remain forever in an external metalanguage, then the supposedly fundamental world is not self-contained. Its order of possibility still depends on operations that never enter its own order of actuality.
